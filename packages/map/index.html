How this works

GlobalGist puts the day's news on a map, where it happened. The map turns until a place lands under the cross. Drag to pick another, or use Find a place (or press /) to go to a place by name. It lists only places with reports today. The panel lists that place's reports, newest first.

The marks

An AI model rates each story's importance from 1 to 5. A place's mark shows its top story: an outline for 1, an outline with a dot for 2, filled for 3, one ring for 4, two rings for 5. A dashed outline is a local story from GDELT, which no model rates. A bigger mark means more reports. The Key on the map shows the marks in your design.

Zoomed out, you see only places with a story rated 4 or 5, or reported from four or more cities. Each step in shows more. Places close together share a mark until you zoom in.

The reports

The reports come from a hand-picked list of outlets in every region. An AI model reads where each story happened, and a program checks that city against a fixed list before placing it. A story with no city stays at its outlet's city. If it happened in another country, it's listed there under "on events elsewhere" and doesn't change that city's mark. Reports, events and the word update once a day.

Where our outlets don't reach, the map adds local stories found through GDELT, an open index of news sites in over 65 languages. That's up to two per town, placed by checking GDELT's town against the city list and GeoNames' towns. No AI reads or rates them. They're marked "via GDELT", show only when you zoom in close, and update every three hours.

The word

Each day an AI model explains up to 25 events rated 3 or more, and scores each from −2 to +2 by what happened to people, never by which side gained. It scores the day three times and keeps each event's middle score. Every score quotes a checked sentence.

A fixed formula, not the model, puts the day on a five-step scale: Severe harm, Harm, Mixed, Relief, Resolution. If any score is below zero, the lowest one sets the day, so good news never cancels a tragedy. Otherwise the day is the average, weighted by importance. The model picks the word from a fixed list for that step. Open the word to see every score.

Explanations

Every sentence in an explanation quotes a source article. A program removes any sentence whose quote isn't in the article word for word.

What we don't do

  • No borders or country names.
  • No ranking of headlines. Lists are newest first. The word is the site's only judgment, and it shows its reasons.
  • No rewritten headlines.
  • No copied articles. You see the outlet's own summary and a link.

Translation

Translate shows headlines in a language you pick. Your browser translates them when it can. Otherwise only the headline text goes to our host, Cloudflare, to translate. Machine translations are labelled and can be wrong.

Privacy

No cookies, analytics or accounts. Your settings and pins stay in this browser. Pictures and articles you open load from their outlets.
