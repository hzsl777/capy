How this works

Capy puts the world's news on a map by where it is published. Turn the map; the place under the crosshair is what you're tuned to, and the panel lists what its outlets reported, newest first.

Where the reports come from

A hand-picked list of outlets from every region. Each outlet is a pin at the city it publishes from, the way Radio Garden places stations. Nothing is placed by guessing where an event happened, so the map never draws or names a disputed place. The map is updated once a day.

The word at the top

Each day an AI model reads the day's conflict reporting and sums it up in one word, then lists the events that word stands for. The word is checked by a program before it is published: it must appear in the verified sentences about those events, it must not name a place, person or side, and it must not be a contested or alarm word. When nothing rises above routine, the word is "Quiet".

Explanations and sources

Some events have an explanation written by the same model. Every sentence must quote a passage from one of the source articles, and a program checks that the passage is really there, word for word. Sentences that fail are removed before anything is published. Each sentence links to its source and the quoted passage.

What we don't do

  • No borders and no country names. The map shows coastlines, rivers, lakes, mountains and ice.
  • No ranking of headlines. Lists are newest first. The only choice made for you is the day's word and its events, and you can see exactly what it rests on.
  • No rewriting of headlines. They appear as the outlet published them.
  • No copied articles. The panel shows the outlet's own feed summary and links to the full story.

Translation

Translate uses your browser's built-in, on-device translator where available. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Map data: Natural Earth (public domain). Headlines, summaries and articles belong to their publishers.
