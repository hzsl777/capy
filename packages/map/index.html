How this works

Capy puts the world's news on a map by place. Turn the map; whatever sits under the crosshair is what you're tuned to.

Where the reports come from

Articles come from the GDELT Project, a free open dataset that monitors news outlets worldwide in more than 65 languages, plus a short list of hand-picked local outlets. The map is updated about once an hour.

How a story gets its dot

Each article is placed at the first city or province it mentions, which is usually its dateline. Articles that only name a country are left off, since a dot in the middle of a country isn't a real place. Placement is automatic and sometimes wrong.

What we don't do

  • No borders and no country names. The map shows coastlines, rivers, lakes, mountains and ice.
  • No ranking. Stories are listed newest first. Nothing is ordered by popularity, tone or "importance".
  • No rewriting. Headlines appear as the outlet wrote them. There are no generated summaries.
  • No single outlet can fill a place: outlets take turns, and each one is capped across the map.

Reading in the app

The reader shows the headline, image and short description the outlet publishes for link previews. If the outlet allows it, you can open the full page inside the app; otherwise read it on the outlet's site. Full articles are never copied here.

Translation

Translate uses your browser's built-in, on-device translator where available. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Map data: Natural Earth (public domain). News index: GDELT Project. Headlines and previews belong to their publishers.
