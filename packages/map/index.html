How this works

GlobalGist puts the world's news on a map by where it is published. When you open it, the map turns until a place lands under the cross in the middle. Drag to turn it yourself. The panel lists what that place's outlets reported, newest first.

What shows at each zoom

Zoomed out, the map shows places with a story reported from three or more places, or one the AI model rated 4 or 5 for importance. Zoom in and the rest appear. Nearby places merge into one dot until you zoom in far enough to separate them. A dot's size depends only on how many reports it holds.

Where the reports come from

The reports come from a hand-picked list of outlets in every region. Each outlet is a pin at the city it publishes from, the way Radio Garden places stations. The site never guesses where an event happened, so the map never draws or names a disputed place. The data updates once a day.

The word at the top

Each day an AI model scores each explained world event from −2 to +2 by what happened to people. Loss of life is at one end. Lives saved or a lasting agreement is at the other. The model scores outcomes, never which side gained. It scores the day three times and each event keeps its middle score, so one odd answer can't change the word. Every score quotes the checked sentence it rests on.

A formula, not the model, then places the day on a five-step scale from Grave to Good. When a significant event scored below zero, the worst of those events sets the day. Good news never averages a tragedy away. The model picks the word from a fixed list for that step, and a program rejects any other word. Open the word to see every score and its reason.

Explanations and sources

The same model writes an explanation for some events. Every sentence must quote a passage from a source article. A program checks that the passage is in the article, word for word, and removes any sentence that fails. Each sentence links to its source and the quoted passage.

What we don't do

  • No borders and no country names. The map shows coastlines, rivers, lakes, mountains and ice.
  • No ranking of headlines. Lists are newest first. The day's word and its scores are the only judgment the site makes, and each one shows what it rests on.
  • No rewritten headlines. They appear as the outlet published them.
  • No copied articles. The panel shows the outlet's own feed summary and links to the full story.

Translation

Translate uses your browser's built-in translator, which runs on your device. Nothing is sent anywhere. Browsers without one can't translate.
