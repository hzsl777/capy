# GlobalGist (the map): agent guide

The map, published as GlobalGist, is one of two products in capy. The other is 2DayAI (see the root AGENTS.md and CONTEXT.md). GlobalGist is the public site. It puts world news on a map by where it happens (decision 44), in the spirit of Radio Garden. One word heads it: the emotion the day's world reporting evokes, on a scored scale from Severe harm to Resolution (decision 59).

On open, the map or globe turns until a place lands under the small reticle in the middle; drag to turn it yourself, and when you let go the nearest place within reach glides under the reticle (decision 125). The place under the reticle is "tuned", and the side panel lists what its outlets reported. Opening the word shows:

- the scale and the events that shaped the day
- every event's score with the sentence it rests on
- each event's explanation with numbered citations
- the sources with the passages quoted

The data comes from the shared pipeline (decision 25): world-desk sources in `config/sources.yaml`, the `cluster world`, `explain` and `telegram` stages, and the `loadMapView` read model in `packages/db`. The Worker (`packages/web`) serves this site's build and its data at `/data/latest.json`, with the day's GDELT local stories in tiles at `/data/local/<date>/<tile>.json` (decision 78). This package holds the site only.

## Neutrality rules (hard rules)

The site is public and covers contested places. These rules apply to every change. Run the `neutrality-review` skill before finishing any change to the UI, the basemap, the telegram, or how world events are chosen.

1. **No political geography.** The basemap has land, coastlines, lakes, rivers, relief and ice. No borders, no disputed-area lines, no country fills, no country names anywhere in the UI. Never add a Natural Earth `admin_*` or `boundary_*` layer.
2. **No labels on the map, and a story sits where it happened, checked** (decision 44). The map shows dots only, one per city. A place name appears only in the panel, never with a country. The one headline set beside a place on the map is Couch Potato's card above the reticle (decision 100): the tuned place's headlines from the panel, one at a time, with the outlet and the time and no place or country name, for a single place only (never a merged dot) and never while the map turns or the reader is open. The grouping model names the city where an event happened; code places it only when that city is on the fixed city list (`packages/pipeline/data/places.json`, from Natural Earth) or lies within 250 km of a listed city in the country the model named. Anything else, including a story that names only a country or a region, stays at its outlet's city. The model may take the city from an institution the articles name whose seat is one city (a named court, parliament, ministry, exchange, or a company's headquarters for its own announcement), and gives the event's country even with no city (cluster-world.v4, decision 107); the country is never shown. A report left at its outlet's city about another country is marked `abroad`: the panel lists it under "From outlets in X, on events elsewhere", it never sets that city's mark or zoom level, and Couch Potato's card leaves it out. Nothing places a story at a country or region, and every report shows its outlet's own city. The one other source of places is GDELT's city tag on local stories (rule 6), the most-named city of the country the article names most, and none when that country has no city named (decision 93): a town on the list or on GeoNames' list of places of 1,000 people or more and seats of local government (`packages/pipeline/data/towns.txt`, 164,257 towns, CC BY 4.0, credited in the About dialog, decisions 67 and 78) with the same name within 30 km of GDELT's point, or else GDELT's own town within 250 km of a listed city in its country, and nothing coarser. The town list never places the grouping model's stories.
3. **Lists are never ranked; the map is, openly** (decisions 30 and 46). Lists are newest first. A place's list is split by where each outlet is based, and only by that (decision 98): outlets that publish from the place first, then outlets elsewhere that reported on it, then the place's outlets' reports on events in another country (decision 107), then GDELT's local sites, each group newest first (`byOrigin` in `src/data.ts`). No outlet is ranked above another, and importance never orders a list. The map ranks places by the grouping model's 1 to 5 importance and by how many outlet cities reported a story, and only in two ways: which places show at each zoom (`tierOf` in `src/data.ts`, five tiers from importance 4 or 5, or four outlet cities, at the whole world down to everything when zoomed in; GDELT's local stories, which no model rates, only in the last), and each dot's symbol and size (`weightOf`, the place's most important story, sets the symbol: hollow for 1 and GDELT stories, filled for 2 and 3, ringed for 4 and 5, decision 57; the report count sets the size). The Key button on the map opens a panel beside it that explains the symbols; marker shape (circle, square, diamond, bevelled disc, sewn button, hexagon, lily pad, four-, five- or six-point star, flower, gumdrop, shield, model house, hand-drawn circle, X, map pin, order ticket, ticket stub, teacup, candy nugget; `src/map/marks.ts`, decisions 72, 76, 96, 102 and 103) is part of a design and carries no meaning. A marker's size never changes with a camera's distance. Nearby places merge into one dot that lists every city by name, never a region. The "fresh" colour means "reported in the last hour" and nothing else. The mood score is the only sentiment signal. It never orders headlines or changes how a pin looks, and the site always shows every score with its reason.
4. **No unverified text.** Headlines appear as published, less any emoji an outlet set beside them (decision 127): flags and pictographs are taken out on load (`withoutEmoji` in `src/data.ts`), every word, digit and sign stays. Generated text appears in two places only, both built from sentences checked against the sources and both labelled as written by AI: the telegram (the word, the event scores and one line per event) and event explanations. Translation is opt-in, into a language the reader picks (their browser's languages first), and every translated headline or summary is marked "Machine translated from X" (decision 97). The browser's own translator is used when it already has the language pair; otherwise the Worker translates the text alone with Cloudflare Workers AI (`/api/translate`) and keeps it at the edge. Nothing about the reader goes with it, and a headline that could not be translated says so and stays as published.
5. **A formula sets the word, and code checks it** (decision 26, `packages/core/src/world.ts`). Scores are outcomes for people, never which side gained. Each score quotes a verified sentence (`scoreProblems`). `dayBand` lets the worst significant event set a bad day, so good news never averages a tragedy away. The word must come from the band's fixed list, and a bad day must name the event that set it (`wordProblems`). Never loosen these checks to get a word out. A day without a word is acceptable.
6. **Balance by curation and caps.** The world source list is kept balanced across regions and never adds one side of a conflict without the other. Outlets on the sides of an armed conflict or contested place carry `balance: { group, side }` in `config/sources.yaml`; on a day any side of a group has no story, `cluster world` leaves out the whole group, so the map never shows one side's local outlets alone (both or neither, decision 90). International outlets still cover the place, and events are pinned where they happened. `cluster world` keeps at most `WORLD_PER_SOURCE` (default 15) articles per source per day. Local stories from the GDELT index (decisions 54, 67 and 78) add towns the outlets did not reach: the newest `GDELT_PER_TOWN` (default 2) of every town GDELT tags in the last 24 hours, and `GDELT_MAX` (default 80,000) in all, a safety valve set above a normal day. The daily run reads the day's window; between daily runs they are read again every three hours from the last 24 hours (decision 80). The refresh also adds the outlets' stories of the day under way, grouped by the model, beside the finished day's (decision 130); the finished day's events and the word stay the daily run's. Every town's newest story comes before any town's second; over the day's limit, towns in a region no listed outlet reached come first within a round, then the newest. Time and place decide, never GDELT's tone or themes. No model reads them, they sit at the lowest zoom tier, and each says "via GDELT". A town within 3 km of a place with an outlet's story is that place and gets none, so an outlet's own town keeps its outlets' stories alone, while the next municipality gets its own; outlet research continues so GDELT fills less over time. They travel in tiles the site loads only at the closest zoom (decision 78), which changes when they load, never which show.
7. **No full article text on the site.** The panel shows the outlet's own feed summary (at most 300 characters). Explanations quote short passages as citations. Article text fetched for verification (decision 16) is never published whole.
8. **Neutral copy.** UI text is plain and descriptive. No adjectives about events or places. No em dashes in UI copy or docs.

## Commands

Run from the repository root:

```
npm install
npm run map:sample       fictional world day through the real stages, in memory, into public/data/sample.json
npm run map:dev          dev server on the sample (http://localhost:5173)
npm run check            boundaries, typecheck and tests for every package, including the map
npm run map:build        typecheck + production build into packages/map/dist/
npm run map:basemap      rebuild the basemap (needs raw.githubusercontent.com)
npm run map:build && npm run map:screenshots
npm run web:deploy       build and deploy with the Worker (needs Cloudflare credentials)
```

To look at a real day locally: `npm run stage -- map export --out packages/map/public/data/latest.json` with `DATABASE_URL` set, then `npm run map:dev`. `latest.json` and the tiles it writes to `public/data/local/<date>/` are gitignored. `npx tsx packages/pipeline/scripts/synthetic-day.ts 50000 --out packages/map/public/data` writes a made-up day of 50,000 local stories there instead, to check speed.

Before pushing: `npm run check && npm run map:build`.

## Data contract

`MapFile` in `packages/core/src/map.ts`, built by `loadMapView` in `packages/db/src/map.ts`. Change the type first, then the read model, then the site.

Local stories are not in the day's file (decision 78). `splitLocal` in core cuts them into tiles on a fixed 10-degree grid of longitude and latitude (`tileKey`, "40N_80W" is 40 to 50 north, 80 to 70 west; empty cells get no file), and the file keeps the outlets' stories, the events, the word and `local`: the grid size, the tiles' folder and each tile's story count. A tile (`MapTile`) has its own places and compact rows. The site asks for the tiles in view (`MapView.cellInView`) only once `level()` reaches the last tier, where GDELT stories show, and never before the first paint. `mergeTiles` in `src/data.ts` joins a tile's places by id and appends new ones, so every place index the page holds (tuned, pinned, highlighted) stays valid, then `refreshDots` redraws. Designs that cache a picture key it on the places (`anchors`), so places added later appear in every design. A pin or a shared link to a town in a tile not loaded yet flies to the point its id names, at the closest zoom, and the tile loads as it lands (`flyToId` in `main.ts`). `map export` writes the tiles to `local/<date>/` beside the day's file; the sample keeps them in `public/data/local/sample/`. The site imports core with `import type` only, so nothing from core (zod included) is bundled. Times are unix seconds. `recent` lists the words before the one shown, the 7 newest days with a word (date, word and band only, newest first), shown as plain text under "Earlier" in the word's view (decision 112). The site measures time windows from `generatedAt`: the end of the day's window, or the newest local story once the refresh during the day has read past it (decision 80), and never later than the export. `source: "sample"` (the fictional day) and `"demo"` (headlines gathered outside the daily run) show a one-line banner. `"live"` never does. `web:deploy` removes `sample.json` from the build so the public site can never fall back to it.

## Conventions

- TypeScript, ES modules, no framework. Keep dependencies small.
- Third-party text (headlines, summaries, outlet names, quoted passages) goes into the DOM as text via `h()`. URLs go through `safeUrl()`.
- Comments explain why, not what.
